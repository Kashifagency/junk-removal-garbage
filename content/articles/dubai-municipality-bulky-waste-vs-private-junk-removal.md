Got an old sofa, a broken washing machine or a room full of clutter? In Dubai you have two main options: the **free bulky waste service from Dubai Municipality**, or a **private junk removal company**. The free service is genuinely useful — but it doesn't cover every home, every item or every deadline. This guide explains who qualifies, what's excluded, and when private **bulky waste removal in Dubai** is the faster (or only) option.

## What is the Dubai Municipality bulky waste service?

Dubai Municipality runs a **free bulky waste collection service** for used household items that are too large for normal bins. According to the [municipality's own announcement](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-municipality-promotes-waste-segregation-through-free-bulky-waste-disposal-service/), it covers:

- Used household furniture
- Electrical appliances
- Electronics

Requests are made via **WhatsApp on 800900**. [Khaleej Times reports](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/dubai-offers-free-bulk-waste-removal-service-via-whatsapp) that the municipality then schedules the collection within three days and confirms it by SMS.

> **Note:** The free service is run by Dubai Municipality, not by us. Always check the latest conditions on the municipality's official channels before relying on it.

## Who can't use the free service?

This is where many residents get caught out. Dubai Municipality states that **residents of real estate development zones and free zones cannot apply** for the free service. Many of Dubai's popular freehold communities fall into development zones — so if you live in a master-planned community, check with your building or community management first.

The service also **excludes some types of waste**, including:

- Home maintenance and renovation waste
- Agricultural and garden waste
- Bathroom fixtures such as sinks and toilets

Private collection makes sense when the free service doesn't fit your situation:

- **You live in a freehold or development-zone community** — for example many towers in [Dubai Marina](/dubai-marina/), apartments in [Jumeirah Village Circle](/jumeirah-village-circle/), or villas on [Palm Jumeirah](/palm-jumeirah/) and in [Arabian Ranches](/arabian-ranches/).

- **It's a business.** Offices, shops and warehouses need [commercial waste management](/services/commercial-waste-management/).

## Living in an apartment tower? Plan the logistics first

Most bulky waste headaches in Dubai aren't about the item — they're about getting it out of the building. Before any collection, private or municipal:

- **Tell building management.** Many towers ask residents to book the service lift and notify security before large items are moved. Some only allow moves at set times.
- **Book the service lift.** Sofas, wardrobes and mattresses rarely fit in passenger lifts, and moving them that way can break building rules.
- **Check parking and loading.** Find out where a pickup truck can stop — a loading bay, basement ramp or visitor parking — so the crew isn't carrying items a long way.
- **Protect shared areas.** Corridors and lift interiors can be scratched by heavy furniture. A good crew wraps items and takes care in common areas.

> **Tip:** Ask building management whether they need the removal company's details in advance. Having this sorted before collection day avoids delays at the gate.

## What to prepare before you book

A little preparation makes any collection faster and keeps the price accurate:

1. **Make a quick list** of everything that needs to go — furniture, appliances, boxes, renovation leftovers.
2. **Take clear photos** of the items, including anything stored on balconies or in storerooms.
3. **Note the access details:** floor number, lift availability, and any stairs.
4. **Separate anything you're keeping** so nothing goes by mistake.
5. **Empty and defrost appliances** like fridges and freezers a day before, and disconnect washing machines.

Sending photos and access details on WhatsApp means you'll know the price before anyone arrives — no surprises on the day.

## Don't leave items on the street

Whichever option you choose, never leave furniture or waste beside a building or on vacant land. Dumping waste in public places is a fined offence under Dubai's waste regulations — see [Executive Council Resolution No. 14 of 2015](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2015/Executive%20Council%20Resolution%20No.%20(14)%20of%202015.html) and the newer [Law No. 18 of 2024 regulating waste management](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2024/Law%20No.%20(18)%20of%202024%20Regulating%20Waste%20Management.html).
